# Historical Analysis and Combinations Requested Later

## Interaction and result categories
When the user requests charts or trend analysis (画图 / 分析趋势), read the latest committed data and existing combinations and produce applicable default charts. Do not make preference questions a blocker. Optionally ask: “Are there combinations you did not set up earlier but would now like to buy together? Just name the items; I will distinguish observations from estimates.” Continue default analysis without an answer.
For a new combination, map exact product identity versions (real SKUs or evidence-backed single-variant substitute identifiers), specifications, and quantities. Explain that no historical observed combination quotes exist and first assess reconstructability. Adding a combination to future observed captures requires explicit confirmation of its definition and selection scope; a historical-analysis request does not automatically expand future capture scope.

## Comparability first
Group by verified product identity version, original quantity, currency, region/account eligibility/membership/channel, and price semantics. Do not call a lower line total caused by a quantity change a price drop. Do not join different identity versions into one product curve without evidence. Label the observation window when comparing observations taken at different times. Curves are not transaction prices or future guarantees.
Classify results as OBSERVED_SINGLE, OBSERVED_COMBO, RECONSTRUCTED, SCENARIO, TEMPORAL_IMPUTED, or NOT_IDENTIFIABLE, consistently across legends, columns, and narrative. Do not write unobserved combinations into ComboQuotes.

## Algorithm for reconstructing arbitrary historical combinations
1. Map the requested combination to evidence-supported product identity versions and quantities at each historical time. Missing rows, unknown quantity-price tiers, or delisted/unselectable items do not justify assuming continued purchasability; mark unquotable/unknown.
2. Select the **base prices that do not double-count discounts** for that time and context. Never sum already-coupon-discounted individual quotes and apply a threshold discount again, or infer unknown original prices backward. Unknown base prices prevent unique identification.
3. Retrieve all relevant historical rule versions, mappings, coupon eligibility/remaining uses, validity, calculation bases, exclusions, and application order. Rules need evidence from that time; never apply today's promotion to yesterday. Identical wording does not automatically identify the same rule.
4. Build scope sets (item, shop, specified set, cross-store, platform) and evaluate eligibility. Output YES/NO/UNKNOWN and evidence for each rule. Failing a threshold means the rule did not trigger for that quote, not that it should be deleted.
5. Form a dependency graph of rules known to combine. Follow evidenced order to calculate bases, thresholds, repeated-threshold counts/caps, multi-item discounts, and rounding units. Treat cycles or unknown order, or unknown allocation affecting subsequent thresholds, as non-unique. Calculate in integer minor currency units and retain each step's inputs/outputs and known platform rounding rules.
6. Enumerate valid promotion choices only within known rule permissions, not all cart-item combinations. Enforce eligibility, exclusions, use limits, and validity. Do not include a coupon in an already-eligible result without evidence it was claimed; optionally show a separate hypothetical “if claimed and applicable at that time” scenario.
7. If important variables such as shipping, region, or membership are unknown, report the known merchandise amount and missing inputs; do not default shipping to zero. For non-unique results, show explicit scenarios or provable bounds. Do not invent ranges or probabilities without reliable bounds.
8. Save input observation IDs, rule versions, context, engine_version, trace, missing inputs, and assumptions in ComboAnalysis. Distinguish at least fully reconstructable, conditional estimate, and unreconstructable. “Fully reconstructable” remains a model reconstruction, not an actual selection observation.
9. Where an observed quote exists for the same historical combination and context, compare the modeled and observed amounts, record residuals, and investigate omissions. Never alter raw rules to force agreement; one matching result does not establish validity for every period.

By default, each historical point is an independent “if purchased then” counterfactual, not a sequence of orders consuming coupons. A sequential-purchasing strategy requires a separate coupon-balance/purchase-state model; do not repeatedly consume one coupon and call the strategy executable.

## Temporal imputation (distinct from rule reconstruction)
Generate PriceEstimates only on an explicit user request. Preserve raw gaps. Mean, linear, or last-observation-carried-forward methods estimate prices, not actual flash offers, coupon eligibility, or threshold changes. Record the method, real observations on either side, gap, assumptions, and uncertainty. By default, do not impute long gaps or periods near promotion transitions. Do not provide numerical confidence intervals without statistical validation.
If reconstruction uses imputed inputs, label it “combination estimate containing temporal imputation,” not merely “rule reconstruction.” Report real coverage and the imputed share; do not mistake missing-data-induced smoothness for market stability.

## Interpretation boundaries
Report minima/maxima and changes only within the observed window, not an all-time historical low. Associated promotions and price changes do not establish causality. State the latest observation time and whether it is stale. When asked when to buy, compare known facts and conditions without guaranteeing future lows or offer availability.

## Analysis without SKU IDs and time axes
Both VERIFIED_SINGLE_VARIANT and VERIFIED_SKU support price curves and rule reconstruction when evidence, specifications/packaging, quantity, and quote context are consistent. Do not require non-null sku_id. Real row-level observations for UNRESOLVED_VARIANT can still appear as separate unlinked points; mark only historical joins/reconstructions dependent on that unresolved identity NOT_IDENTIFIABLE, without blocking other items. Apply identity upgrades only within mapping events' supported effective ranges; never rewrite or invent history.
Declare analysis_timezone for each chart/table (default: the currently effective user timezone). Sort by UTC and display full offsets to distinguish repeated daylight-saving times. Display conversion must not change original observation times; timezone changes do not imply price-context or delivery-region changes. Calculate coverage against the planned periods effective under each schedule_revision, not by retroactively imposing a new frequency on old periods. Imputation still requires a separate user request.
